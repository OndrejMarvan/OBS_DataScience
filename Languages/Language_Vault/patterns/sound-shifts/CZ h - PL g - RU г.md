# CZ h ↔ PL g ↔ RU г

## Pattern Description
Proto-Slavic *g was preserved in Polish and Russian, but shifted to h in Czech. This is one of the most regular and productive patterns across Slavic languages.

## Direction
**Proto-Slavic *g** → CZ **h** / PL **g** / RU **г** [g]

## Examples
| 🇨🇿 Czech | 🇵🇱 Polish | 🇷🇺 Russian | 🇬🇧 English | Meaning |
|-----------|------------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| hora | góra | гора | mountain | mountain |
| noha | noga | нога | leg/foot | leg |
| hlava | głowa | голова | head | head |
| had | gad | гад | snake/reptile | snake |
| hrách | groch | горох | pea | pea |
| hrom | grom | гром | thunder | thunder |
| host | gość | гость | guest | guest |
| hrob | grób | гроб | grave | grave |
| hrad | gród | город | castle/city | castle |
| hvězda | gwiazda | звезда | star | star |
| hlad | głód | голод | hunger | hunger |
| hřib | grzyb | гриб | mushroom | mushroom |
| holub | gołąb | голубь | pigeon | pigeon |
| hodina | godzina | — | hour | hour |
| hnízdo | gniazdo | гнездо | nest | nest |

## Exceptions / Irregularities
- Not all CZ 'h' comes from *g — some words have original h (borrowed words)
- RU звезда (star) breaks the pattern — it has з instead of г (from *gvězda → звезда with metathesis)
- Some words don't have cognates in all three (e.g., CZ hodina / PL godzina but RU час)

## Historical Note
The shift *g → h happened in Czech around the 13th-14th century. It's part of a broader lenition process. Polish and Russian preserved the original *g sound. This is why Czech stands out from other Slavic languages in this regard.

## Memory Hook 🪝
**"Czech softens, Polish and Russian keep it hard"** — think of Czech as the "gentle" Slavic language that turned hard g into breathy h.

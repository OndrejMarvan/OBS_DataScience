# Verbs of Motion — Slavic Special System

## The Concept
Slavic languages have PAIRS of motion verbs: **determinate** (one direction, one time) and **indeterminate** (habitual, no specific direction). German and English don't distinguish this.

## Core Motion Verb Pairs

### On foot
| | 🇨🇿 det → indet | 🇵🇱 det → indet | 🇷🇺 det → indet | Meaning |
|--|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| go | jít → chodit | iść → chodzić | идти → ходить | walk/go |

| Usage | CZ | PL | RU | EN |
|-------|----|----|----|----|
| Right now, one direction | Jdu do školy. | Idę do szkoły. | Я иду в школу. | I'm going to school. |
| Habitually | Chodím do školy. | Chodzę do szkoły. | Я хожу в школу. | I go to school (regularly). |
| Round trip completed | Chodil jsem v Praze. | Chodziłem po Pradze. | Я ходил по Праге. | I walked around Prague. |

### By vehicle
| | 🇨🇿 det → indet | 🇵🇱 det → indet | 🇷🇺 det → indet |
|--|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| ride/drive | jet → jezdit | jechać → jeździć | ехать → ездить |

| Usage | CZ | PL | RU |
|-------|----|----|----| 
| Going now (one direction) | Jedu do Prahy. | Jadę do Warszawy. | Я еду в Москву. |
| Regularly | Jezdím do Prahy. | Jeżdżę do Warszawy. | Я езжу в Москву. |

> **Russian has MORE pairs than CZ/PL** — including катить/катать (roll) and тащить/таскать (drag). Russian is the most elaborated system.
> **CZ and PL are nearly identical** in which pairs they have and how they work.

## With Prefixes: Motion Verbs + Direction

When you add a prefix to a motion verb, the determinate becomes **perfective** and the indeterminate becomes the **new imperfective**:

| Prefix | CZ perf (from det) | CZ impf (from indet) | Meaning |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|---------|
| při- | přijít | přicházet | to arrive (on foot) |
| od- | odejít | odcházet | to leave (on foot) |
| vy- | vyjít | vycházet | to exit |
| v-/vej- | vejít | vcházet | to enter |
| pře- | přejít | přecházet | to cross |
| pro- | projít | procházet | to pass through |
| ob- | obejít | obcházet | to go around |
| do- | dojít | docházet | to reach/arrive |

| Prefix | PL perf | PL impf | Meaning |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| przy- | przyjść | przychodzić | to arrive |
| od- | odejść | odchodzić | to leave |
| wy- | wyjść | wychodzić | to exit |
| w- | wejść | wchodzić | to enter |
| prze- | przejść | przechodzić | to cross |
| prze- | przejść | przechodzić | to pass through |
| ob- | obejść | obchodzić | to go around |
| do- | dojść | dochodzić | to reach |

> The prefix system is IDENTICAL between CZ and PL! při-/przy-, od-/od-, vy-/wy-, pře-/prze-. Same prefixes, same meanings, same grammar.

## How German/English Handle This

They DON'T have the det/indet distinction. They use context or different verbs:

| Slavic distinction | German | English |
|-------------------|--------|---------|
| jdu (now, one way) | ich gehe (gerade) | I'm going |
| chodím (regularly) | ich gehe (regelmäßig) | I go (regularly) |
| přijdu (will arrive) | ich komme (an) | I'll come/arrive |
| přicházím (am arriving) | ich komme | I'm coming |

> For German/English speakers, motion verbs are THE hardest part of Slavic grammar. For you, they're natural — just map the CZ system 1:1 to PL and RU!

# Verb Aspect — Your Slavic Superpower

Aspect (vid) is the feature that makes Slavic languages unique — and your biggest advantage as a Czech speaker learning Polish and Russian. German and English simply DON'T have it.

## The Core Concept
Every Slavic verb has TWO forms:
- **Imperfective (nedokonavý/niedokonany/несовершенный)** = ongoing, repeated, habitual
- **Perfective (dokonavý/dokonany/совершенный)** = completed, one-time, result

## Aspect Pairs — Same Prefixes Across Languages!

| 🇨🇿 impf → perf | 🇵🇱 impf → perf | 🇷🇺 impf → perf | Meaning |
|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| psát → **na**psat | pisać → **na**pisać | писать → **на**писать | to write |
| učit se → **na**učit se | uczyć się → **na**uczyć się | учить → **на**учить | to learn/teach |

## When to Use Which Aspect

| Situation | Use | 🇨🇿 Example | 🇵🇱 Example |
|-----------|-----|-------------|-------------|
| Ongoing now | IMPF | Píšu dopis. | Piszę list. |
| Habitual/repeated | IMPF | Každý den píšu. | Codziennie piszę. |
| Completed (past) | PERF | Napsal jsem dopis. | Napisałem list. |
| Will complete (future) | PERF | Napíšu dopis. | Napiszę list. |
| Will be doing (future) | IMPF | Budu psát dopis. | Będę pisać list. |
| Request (polite) | IMPF | Můžeš mi psát? | Możesz do mnie pisać? |
| Request (one-time) | PERF | Napiš mi! | Napisz do mnie! |
| Negation (general) | IMPF | Nepsal jsem. | Nie pisałem. |

## How German/English Handle Aspect
They DON'T have aspect — they use other tools:

| Slavic aspect choice | English equivalent | German equivalent |
|---------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Imperfective (process) | "I was writing" / "I used to write" | "Ich schrieb (gerade)" |
| Perfective (result) | "I wrote (it)" / "I have written" | "Ich habe geschrieben" |
| Impf future | "I will be writing" | "Ich werde schreiben" |
| Perf future | "I will write (and finish)" | "Ich werde schreiben" (same!) |

> This is why German/English learners find aspect IMPOSSIBLE — they have no intuition for it. But as a Czech speaker, you use it automatically every day. Just apply the same logic in Polish and Russian!

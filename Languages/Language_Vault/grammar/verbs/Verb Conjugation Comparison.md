# Verb Conjugation — Cross-Linguistic Comparison

## Present Tense: "to do/make" (dělat/robić/machen/делать/do)

| Person | 🇨🇿 dělat | 🇵🇱 robić | 🇩🇪 machen | 🇷🇺 делать | 🇬🇧 |
|--------|----------|----------|------------|-----------|------|
| I | dělám | robię | mache | делаю | do |
| you (sg) | děláš | robisz | machst | делаешь | do |
| he/she/it | dělá | robi | macht | делает | does |
| we | děláme | robimy | machen | делаем | do |
| you (pl/formal) | děláte | robicie | macht | делаете | do |
| they | dělají | robią | machen | делают | do |

### Pattern Analysis
- **CZ vs PL:** Same conjugation logic, different endings (-ám/-áš vs -ię/-isz). Both have 3 conjugation classes.
- **DE:** Much simpler — stem + ending. Fewer distinct forms (machen=machen for "we" and "they").
- **RU:** Close to CZ/PL in concept — personal endings, but slightly different forms (-ю/-ешь/-ет vs CZ -ám/-áš/-á).
- **EN:** Almost no conjugation (just 3rd person -s). Simplest system.

## "To be" — The Most Important Verb

| Person | 🇨🇿 být | 🇵🇱 być | 🇩🇪 sein | 🇷🇺 быть | 🇬🇧 be |
|--------|--------|--------|----------|---------|------|
| I | jsem | jestem | bin | — | am |
| you (sg) | jsi | jesteś | bist | — | are |
| he/she/it | je | jest | ist | — | is |
| we | jsme | jesteśmy | sind | — | are |
| you (pl) | jste | jesteście | seid | — | are |
| they | jsou | są | sind | — | are |

> ⚠️ **Russian drops "to be" in present tense!** "Я студент" = "I [am] student". No verb needed! This is the BIGGEST difference from CZ/PL/DE/EN.

> **CZ+PL are extremely close:** jsem/jestem, jsi/jesteś, je/jest, jsme/jesteśmy — you can almost hear the same word!

## Past Tense Comparison

Past tense is where Slavic languages shine with a unique system:

### Slavic past tense = L-participle (gender-marked!)
| | 🇨🇿 dělat | 🇵🇱 robić | 🇷🇺 делать |
|--|----------|----------|-----------|
| I (masc) | dělal jsem | robiłem | я делал |
| I (fem) | dělala jsem | robiłam | я делала |
| he | dělal | robił | он делал |
| she | dělala | robiła | она делала |
| it | dělalo | robiło | оно делало |
| we (masc/mixed) | dělali jsme | robiliśmy | мы делали |
| we (fem) | dělaly jsme | robiłyśmy | мы делали |
| they (masc personal) | dělali | robili | они делали |
| they (other) | dělaly | robiły | они делали |

> **Key insight:** All three Slavic languages use L-participle (dělal/robił/делал) + some form of "to be" (CZ has it as auxiliary jsem, PL attaches it as suffix -em, RU drops it entirely!)
> **Gender in past tense** — ALL Slavic languages mark gender in past tense. German and English do NOT.

### German past tense (Perfekt — spoken language)
| Person | haben + Partizip II |
|--------|-------------------|
| ich | habe gemacht |
| du | hast gemacht |
| er/sie/es | hat gemacht |
| wir | haben gemacht |
| ihr | habt gemacht |
| sie/Sie | haben gemacht |

> DE uses "have + past participle" — similar to English "I have done". No gender marking!

## Verb Aspect — Slavic Exclusive Feature

| | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇷🇺 | 🇩🇪 | 🇬🇧 |
|--|------|------|------|------|------|
| System | ✅ dokonavý/nedokonavý | ✅ dokonany/niedokonany | ✅ совершенный/несовершенный | ❌ | ❌ |
| "to write" (impf) | psát | pisać | писать | schreiben | write |
| "to write" (perf) | napsat | napisać | написать | schreiben | write |
| "to read" (impf) | číst | czytać | читать | lesen | read |
| "to read" (perf) | přečíst | przeczytać | прочитать | lesen | read |

> **CZ+PL+RU use the SAME prefixes for perfectivization!** na-psat = na-pisać = на-писать, pře-číst = prze-czytać = про-читать
> German and English simply don't have this. They use adverbs or context ("I wrote" vs "I was writing").

## Conjugation Classes Comparison

### Czech: 5 classes (-at, -it, -ět, -ovat, -nout)
### Polish: 4 classes (-ać, -ić/-yć, -eć, -ować)
### German: Strong (irregular) vs Weak (regular) + Mixed
### Russian: 2 conjugations (1st: -ать/-еть, 2nd: -ить)

> The CZ↔PL mapping is nearly 1:1:
> CZ -at → PL -ać (dělat → działać)
> CZ -it → PL -ić (mluvit → mówić)
> CZ -ovat → PL -ować (pracovat → pracować)

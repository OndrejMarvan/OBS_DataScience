# Numerals and Counting — The Hardest Part of Slavic Grammar

## The Problem
Slavic languages have COMPLEX rules for what case/form the noun takes after different numbers. This trips up even advanced learners.

## The Rules

### 1 = Nominative singular (all languages agree)
| CZ | PL | RU | DE |
|----|----|----|----|
| jeden student | jeden student | один студент | ein Student |
| jedna studentka | jedna studentka | одна студентка | eine Studentin |

### 2, 3, 4 = Special forms
| Language | Rule | Example (2 students) |
|----------|------|---------------------|
| CZ | Nominative plural | dva **studenti** |
| PL | Nominative plural | dwaj/dwóch **studentów** (masc pers: genitive!) |
| RU | Genitive singular! | два **студента** |
| DE | Normal plural | zwei **Studenten** |

> ⚠️ **Polish masculine personal quirk:** For masculine persons with 2-4, Polish uses genitive plural: dwóch **studentów**, trzech **studentów**, czterech **studentów**. Non-personal: dwa **domy**, trzy **domy**.

### 5-20 = Genitive plural (Slavic!)
| Language | Rule | Example (5 students) |
|----------|------|---------------------|
| CZ | Genitive plural | pět **studentů** |
| PL | Genitive plural | pięciu **studentów** |
| RU | Genitive plural | пять **студентов** |
| DE | Normal plural | fünf **Studenten** |

### 21 = Back to nominative (last digit = 1)
| CZ | PL | RU |
|----|----|----|
| dvacet jeden **student** | dwudziestu jeden **studentów** | двадцать один **студент** |

### 22-24 = Same as 2-4 rules
### 25-30 = Same as 5-20 rules (genitive plural)

## Quick Reference

| Number | CZ (rok=year) | PL (rok=year) | RU (год=year) |
|--------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| 1 | jeden **rok** | jeden **rok** | один **год** |
| 2 | dva **roky** | dwa **lata** | два **года** |
| 3 | tři **roky** | trzy **lata** | три **года** |
| 4 | čtyři **roky** | cztery **lata** | четыре **года** |
| 5 | pět **let** (gen pl!) | pięć **lat** | пять **лет** |
| 10 | deset **let** | dziesięć **lat** | десять **лет** |
| 21 | dvacet jeden **rok** | dwadzieścia jeden **lat** | двадцать один **год** |
| 100 | sto **let** | sto **lat** | сто **лет** |

> **"Year" is irregular in ALL Slavic!** CZ rok→let, PL rok→lat, RU год→лет. The genitive plural is from a different root (léto=summer→years).
> The Polish birthday song is "Sto lat!" (100 years!) — same concept as CZ "Sto let!"

## German Is Simple Here
German doesn't change noun forms after numbers AT ALL:
- ein Student, zwei Studenten, fünf Studenten, hundert Studenten
- Just singular for 1, plural for everything else. Done.

## Ordinal Numbers

| # | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇩🇪 | 🇷🇺 |
|---|------|------|------|------|
| 1st | první | pierwszy | erster | первый |
| 2nd | druhý | drugi | zweiter | второй |
| 3rd | třetí | trzeci | dritter | третий |
| 4th | čtvrtý | czwarty | vierter | четвёртый |
| 5th | pátý | piąty | fünfter | пятый |
| 6th | šestý | szósty | sechster | шестой |
| 7th | sedmý | siódmy | siebter | седьмой |
| 8th | osmý | ósmy | achter | восьмой |
| 9th | devátý | dziewiąty | neunter | девятый |
| 10th | desátý | dziesiąty | zehnter | десятый |
| 20th | dvacátý | dwudziesty | zwanzigster | двадцатый |
| 100th | stý | setny | hundertster | сотый |

> CZ+PL+RU ordinals are cognate and follow the same pattern!
> DE just adds -ter/-ster to the cardinal — simplest system.

## Collective Numerals (Slavic only)
CZ, PL, and RU have special collective forms for groups:

| Number | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇷🇺 | Used for |
|--------|------|------|------|---------|
| 2 | dvoje | dwoje | двое | mixed gender groups, pluralia tantum |
| 3 | troje | troje | трое | mixed gender groups |
| 4 | čtvery | czworo | четверо | mixed gender groups |
| 5 | patery | pięcioro | пятеро | mixed gender groups |

> "Dvoje dětí" (CZ) = "dwoje dzieci" (PL) = "двое детей" (RU) = "two children (mixed or unspecified gender)"
> German and English have nothing like this!

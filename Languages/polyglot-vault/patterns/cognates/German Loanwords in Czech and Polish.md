# German Loanwords in Czech and Polish

Centuries of contact with German-speaking lands left hundreds of loanwords in both Czech and Polish. These are "free bridges" — words you already know from CZ/PL that connect directly to German.

## Why This Matters for German Learning
1. **You already know the meaning** — you just need to learn the original German pronunciation
2. **The borrowing direction tells you the article:** Many borrowed words kept similar gender
3. **Spelling differences are predictable:** CZ š = DE sch, CZ c = DE z, PL sz = DE sch
4. **Some words stayed, some left:** Not all German loanwords survived in CZ (Czech purists replaced many with native words in the 19th century National Revival)

## Reverse Direction — Czech/Slavic Words in German
| Slavic origin | 🇩🇪 German | Meaning |
|--------------|------------|---------|
| (CZ) robot(a) | Roboter | robot (coined by Karel Čapek) |
| (CZ/PL) pistole | Pistole | pistol (from píšťala = pipe) |
| (Slavic) Grenze | Grenze | border (from granica!) |
| (Slavic) Quark | Quark | curd cheese (from tvaroh/twaróg!) |
| (CZ) Haubitze | Haubitze | howitzer (from houfnice!) |
| (Slavic) Gurke | Gurke | cucumber (from okurka/ogórek!) |

> **Fun fact:** German "Grenze" (border) comes from Slavic "granica"! And "Quark" comes from "tvaroh"! The borrowing went both ways.

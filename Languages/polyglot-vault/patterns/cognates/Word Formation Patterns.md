# Word Formation — How to Build Words Across Languages

Understanding how Slavic languages build words from roots lets you predict and create vocabulary you've never seen before. The patterns are remarkably similar across CZ, PL, and RU.

## 4. Diminutives — Slavic Specialty

All Slavic languages are RICH in diminutives (expressing smallness, affection, or informality):

| Original | 🇨🇿 dim | 🇵🇱 dim | 🇷🇺 dim | Suffix used |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|-------------|
| dům (house) | domek | domek | домик | -ek/-ek/-ик |
| kočka (cat) | kočička | koteczka | кошечка | -ička/-eczka/-ечка |
| kniha (book) | knížka | książka (!) | книжка | -žka/-żka/-жка |
| stůl (table) | stolek | stolik | столик | -ek/-ik/-ик |
| syn (son) | synek | synek | сынок | -ek/-ek/-ок |
| dcera (daughter) | dcerka | córeczka | дочка | -ka/-eczka/-ка |
| pivo (beer) | pivko | piwko | пивко | -ko/-ko/-ко |
| káva (coffee) | kávička | kawusia | кофеёк | varies |

> ⚠️ PL "książka" (book) IS the diminutive form — it became the standard word! Original "księga" now means "tome/ledger".
> Diminutives are used MUCH more in daily Slavic speech than in German or English. Learning them makes you sound natural.
> German has -chen/-lein (Häuschen, Büchlein) but uses them less frequently than Slavic.

## 5. The Compound Word System

### German builds LONG compounds (Slavic doesn't!)

| 🇩🇪 German compound | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | How Slavic handles it |
|---------------------|------|------|---------------------|
| Handschuh (hand-shoe) | rukavice | rękawiczka | separate word (from "ruka/ręka") |
| Kühlschrank (cool-cabinet) | lednička | lodówka | separate word (from "led/lód" = ice) |
| Staubsauger (dust-sucker) | vysavač | odkurzacz | separate word |
| Krankenhaus (sick-house) | nemocnice | szpital | separate word |
| Flugzeug (fly-thing) | letadlo | samolot | separate word |
| Schildkröte (shield-toad) | želva | żółw | separate word |
| Handschuhe (hand-shoes) | rukavice | rękawiczki | from "ruka/ręka" (hand) |
| Krankenwagen (sick-car) | sanitka | karetka | separate word |

> German loves compound nouns — understanding the parts helps enormously:
> Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaftskapitän = Danube-steam-ship-travel-company-captain
> Slavic languages use adjective + noun phrases instead: kapitán dunajské paroplavební společnosti

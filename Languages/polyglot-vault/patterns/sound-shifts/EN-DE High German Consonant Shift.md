# EN ↔ DE High German Consonant Shift

## Pattern Description
The High German consonant shift (Zweite Lautverschiebung) systematically changed consonants from Proto-Germanic to Old High German. Since English descends from Anglo-Saxon (which didn't undergo this shift), the differences are regular and predictable.

## Direction
**Proto-Germanic** → EN preserved / DE shifted

## Pattern 1: t → ss/z
| 🇬🇧 English | 🇩🇪 German | Meaning |
|-------------|------------|---------|
| water | Wasser | water |
| eat | essen | eat |
| street | Straße | street |
| white | weiß | white |
| hate | Hass | hate |
| ten | zehn | ten |
| two | zwei | two |
| tongue | Zunge | tongue |
| to | zu | to |
| tin | Zinn | tin |

## Memory Hook 🪝
**"German hardens and pushes forward"** — consonants generally moved one step "stronger" in German: t→ts/ss, p→pf, d→t, th→d.

When you see a German word that looks almost like English but with a consonant swap — check these patterns first.

## Historical Note
This shift happened around the 3rd-5th centuries CE and is one of the main features that separates High German from Low German and English. Low German (Plattdeutsch), Dutch, and English preserved the older consonants.

# Cases — Cross-Linguistic Comparison

## Summary
Cases are your superpower as a Czech speaker. You already know the most complex case system (7 cases), so every other language is either the same (Polish) or simpler (German 4, Russian 6, English 0).

## Case Inventory

| # | 🇨🇿 Czech | 🇵🇱 Polish | 🇩🇪 German | 🇷🇺 Russian | 🇬🇧 English | Question |
|---|-----------|------------|------------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | Nominativ (1. pád) | Mianownik | Nominativ | Именительный | (subject) | kdo? co? |
| 2 | Genitiv (2. pád) | Dopełniacz | Genitiv | Родительный | of / 's | koho? čeho? |
| 3 | Dativ (3. pád) | Celownik | Dativ | Дательный | to (indirect obj) | komu? čemu? |
| 4 | Akuzativ (4. pád) | Biernik | Akkusativ | Винительный | (direct obj) | koho? co? |
| 5 | Vokativ (5. pád) | Wołacz | ❌ | ❌ (archaic) | — | addressing |
| 6 | Lokál (6. pád) | Miejscownik | ❌ | Предложный | — | o kom? o čem? |
| 7 | Instrumentál (7. pád) | Narzędnik | ❌ | Творительный | with / by | kým? čím? |

### Key differences:
- **CZ = PL:** Same 7 cases, same logic, slightly different endings
- **DE:** Only 4 cases (Nom, Gen, Dat, Acc) — no vocative, locative, or instrumental. Locative+instrumental meanings are handled by prepositions + dative
- **RU:** 6 cases — no vocative (except in old/religious forms). Otherwise very similar to CZ/PL
- **EN:** No case system at all — uses word order and prepositions instead

## Example: "friend" (přítel/przyjaciel/Freund/друг/friend)

| Case | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇩🇪 | 🇷🇺 |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| Nom | přítel | przyjaciel | der Freund | друг |
| Gen | přítele | przyjaciela | des Freundes | друга |
| Dat | příteli | przyjacielowi | dem Freund(e) | другу |
| Acc | přítele | przyjaciela | den Freund | друга |
| Voc | příteli! | przyjacielu! | ❌ | ❌ |
| Loc | o příteli | o przyjacielu | ❌ | о друге |
| Ins | přítelem | przyjacielem | ❌ | другом |

> Notice how CZ and PL are nearly identical in every case form! The endings are slightly different but the pattern is the same.

## Transfer Strategy

### CZ → PL (easiest)
Your Czech cases map 1:1 to Polish. Focus on learning the different endings, not the concept.

| CZ ending pattern | PL ending pattern | Example |
|-------------------|-------------------|---------|
| -u (gen masc) | -u (gen masc) | domu / domu ✅ same |
| -ovi (dat masc) | -owi (dat masc) | příteli**ovi** / przyjaciel**owi** |
| -em (ins masc) | -em (ins masc) | přítel**em** / przyjaciel**em** ✅ same |
| -ě/-e (loc) | -e/-u (loc) | v dom**ě** / w dom**u** ⚠️ different |
| -ách (loc pl) | -ach (loc pl) | v domech / w domach |

### CZ → DE (simplify)
Map your 7 cases down to 4. German merged locative, instrumental, and some other functions into dative:

| CZ case | German equivalent |
|---------|------------------|
| Nominativ → | Nominativ |
| Genitiv → | Genitiv |
| Dativ → | Dativ |
| Akuzativ → | Akkusativ |
| Lokál → | **Dativ** (+ preposition) |
| Instrumentál → | **Dativ** (mit + Dativ) |
| Vokativ → | ❌ (just use nominative) |

### CZ → RU (drop vocative, learn new endings)
| CZ case | RU equivalent |
|---------|---------------|
| Nominativ → | Именительный |
| Genitiv → | Родительный |
| Dativ → | Дательный |
| Akuzativ → | Винительный |
| Vokativ → | ❌ (gone) |
| Lokál → | Предложный (always with preposition) |
| Instrumentál → | Творительный |

## Preposition Case Government

### Comparison of key prepositions
| Meaning | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇩🇪 | 🇷🇺 |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| in/at | v + loc | w + loc | in + dat/acc | в + loc/acc |
| on | na + loc | na + loc | auf + dat/acc | на + loc/acc |
| to (direction) | do + gen | do + gen | nach/zu + dat | в/на + acc |
| from | z + gen | z + gen | aus/von + dat | из/с + gen |
| with | s + ins | z + ins | mit + dat | с + ins |
| without | bez + gen | bez + gen | ohne + acc | без + gen |
| for | pro + acc | dla + gen | für + acc | для + gen |
| about | o + loc | o + loc | über + acc | о + loc |

> **Pattern:** CZ and PL are almost identical in preposition + case combinations!
> DE uses fewer cases but the same preposition logic
> RU is very close to CZ/PL with minor differences

# Passive Voice and Impersonal Constructions

## Passive Voice Formation

### Czech
**být + past participle (trpné příčestí)**
- Dopis **je psán**. (The letter is being written.)
- Dopis **byl napsán**. (The letter was written.)
- Dopis **bude napsán**. (The letter will be written.)

### Polish
**być/zostać + past participle (imiesłów bierny)**
- List **jest pisany**. (is being written — imperfective)
- List **został napisany**. (was written — perfective, more common!)
- List **będzie napisany**. (will be written)

### German
**werden + Partizip II**
- Der Brief **wird geschrieben**. (is being written)
- Der Brief **wurde geschrieben**. (was written)
- Der Brief **wird geschrieben werden**. (will be written)

### Russian
**быть (optional) + краткое причастие (short participle)**
- Письмо **написано**. (The letter is/was written.)
- Письмо **было написано**. (was written — past explicit)
- Письмо **будет написано**. (will be written)

## Comparison Summary

| Feature | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇩🇪 | 🇷🇺 |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| Auxiliary | být | być/zostać | werden | быть (often omitted!) |
| Participle agrees in | gender+number | gender+number | — (no agreement) | gender+number |
| Common in speech? | Rare | Moderate | Common | Moderate |
| Preferred alternative | se-construction | się-construction | man-construction | -ся verbs |

## Reflexive Passive — More Natural in Slavic!

In everyday Slavic, passive is often expressed with **se/się/-ся** (reflexive) instead of the formal participle construction:

| 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇷🇺 | 🇬🇧 |
|------|------|------|------|
| Tady **se** mluví česky. | Tu **się** mówi po polsku. | Здесь **говорят** по-русски. | Czech/Polish/Russian is spoken here. |
| To **se** nedělá. | Tego **się** nie robi. | Так **не делают**. | That's not done. |
| Kniha **se** dobře prodává. | Książka dobrze **się** sprzedaje. | Книга хорошо **продаётся**. | The book sells well. |
| Jak **se** to řekne? | Jak **się** to mówi? | Как это **говорится**? | How do you say this? |

> **CZ se = PL się** — identical reflexive passive construction! This is much more natural than formal passive in both languages.
> RU uses either -ся verbs (продаётся, говорится) or 3rd person plural without subject (говорят = "they say" = "it is said").

## Impersonal Constructions (No Subject)

### "One does..." / "People do..." / "You do..." (general)

| Language | Construction | Example | Literal |
|----------|-------------|---------|---------|
| CZ | se + 3rd sg | **Říká se**, že... | It says itself that... |
| PL | się + 3rd sg | **Mówi się**, że... | It says itself that... |
| DE | **man** + 3rd sg | **Man sagt**, dass... | One says that... |
| RU | 3rd pl (no subject) | **Говорят**, что... | (They) say that... |
| EN | you/one/people | **They say** / **People say**... | — |

### Polish -no/-to Construction (unique!)
Polish has a special impersonal past tense that no other language has:

| PL | CZ equivalent | EN |
|----|--------------|-----|
| **Zrobiono** to wczoraj. | To se udělalo včera. | It was done yesterday. |
| **Powiedziano** mi, że... | Řekli mi, že... | I was told that... |
| **Kupiono** nowy samochód. | Koupilo se nové auto. | A new car was bought. |

> This -no/-to form is uniquely Polish — it has no subject at all, just the action. Czech, Russian, German, and English all need different workarounds.

## "It is necessary" / "One must" — Modal Impersonals

| Meaning | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇩🇪 | 🇷🇺 |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| one must/it's necessary | musí se / je třeba | trzeba / należy | man muss / es ist nötig | нужно / надо / необходимо |
| one can | dá se / lze / je možné | można / da się | man kann / es ist möglich | можно |
| one cannot | nedá se / nelze | nie można / nie da się | man kann nicht | нельзя |
| it's forbidden | je zakázáno / nesmí se | nie wolno / zabrania się | es ist verboten | нельзя / запрещено |
| it's worth | stojí za to | warto | es lohnt sich | стоит |

> **CZ dá se = PL da się** — identical! "It gives itself" = "it's possible"
> **CZ nedá se = PL nie da się** — "it doesn't give itself" = "it's impossible"
> **PL trzeba = CZ je třeba** — cognate! Both mean "it's necessary"
> **RU можно/нельзя** — extremely common pair: "allowed"/"not allowed or impossible"

## "I like" / "I need" — Dative Subject Constructions

Slavic languages often express feelings/needs with DATIVE subject (the experiencer):

| Meaning | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇷🇺 | 🇩🇪 |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| I like (it appeals to me) | **Líbí se mi** to. | **Podoba mi się** to. | **Мне нравится** это. | Das **gefällt mir**. |
| I'm cold | **Je mi** zima. | **Jest mi** zimno. | **Мне** холодно. | **Mir** ist kalt. |
| I'm hot | **Je mi** horko. | **Jest mi** gorąco. | **Мне** жарко. | **Mir** ist heiß. |
| I need | Potřebuji... | Potrzebuję... | **Мне нужно**... | Ich brauche... |
| I feel like | Chce se mi... | Chce mi się... | **Мне хочется**... | Ich habe Lust... |
| I'm sorry | Je mi líto. | Jest mi przykro/żal. | **Мне жаль**. | Es tut **mir** leid. |

> **Dative experiencer** is shared by ALL your languages including German! "Mir ist kalt" = "Мне холодно" = "Je mi zima" — same construction with dative!
> This is one of the few grammar points where German works like Slavic languages.

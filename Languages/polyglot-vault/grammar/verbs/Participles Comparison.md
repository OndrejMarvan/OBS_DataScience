# Participles — Cross-Linguistic Comparison

## What Are Participles?
Participles are verb forms used as adjectives or in compound tenses. Slavic languages have a RICH participle system; German has fewer; English has the fewest.

## Active Present Participle (doing)

| Language | Formation | Example: "reading" | Used as adjective |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| CZ | -oucí/-ící | čtoucí / čtoucí člověk | ✅ (literary) |
| PL | -ący/-ący | czytający / czytający człowiek | ✅ (common) |
| DE | -end | lesend / der lesende Mensch | ✅ (common) |
| RU | -ущий/-ющий/-ащий/-ящий | читающий / читающий человек | ✅ (very common) |
| EN | -ing | reading / the reading person | ✅ |

### Examples across languages
| Meaning | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇩🇪 | 🇷🇺 |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| sleeping child | spící dítě | śpiące dziecko | das schlafende Kind | спящий ребёнок |
| running water | tekoucí voda | bieżąca woda | fließendes Wasser | текущая вода |
| interesting book | zajímavá kniha | interesująca książka | ein interessantes Buch | интересная книга |
| following day | následující den | następujący dzień | der folgende Tag | следующий день |

> **PL -ący = RU -ущий/-ящий** — both are very productive and common in everyday language.
> **CZ -oucí/-ící** is more literary — Czech often prefers relative clauses (který čte) instead.

## Passive Past Participle (done)

| Language | Formation | Example: "written" | Used for passive |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| CZ | -ný/-tý/-ený | napsaný / napsaný dopis | ✅ |
| PL | -ny/-ty/-ony | napisany / napisany list | ✅ |
| DE | ge-...-t / ge-...-en | geschrieben / der geschriebene Brief | ✅ |
| RU | -нный/-тый/-ённый | написанный / написанное письмо | ✅ |

### Examples
| Meaning | 🇨🇿 | 🇵🇱 | 🇩🇪 | 🇷🇺 |
|---------|------|------|------|------|
| written letter | napsaný dopis | napisany list | der geschriebene Brief | написанное письмо |
| closed door | zavřené dveře | zamknięte drzwi | die geschlossene Tür | закрытая дверь |
| cooked food | uvařené jídlo | ugotowane jedzenie | das gekochte Essen | приготовленная еда |
| broken window | rozbité okno | rozbite okno | das zerbrochene Fenster | разбитое окно |
| lost key | ztracený klíč | zgubiony klucz | der verlorene Schlüssel | потерянный ключ |

> **CZ -ný = PL -ny** — nearly identical endings!
> CZ napsaný = PL napisany, CZ zavřený = PL zamknięty, CZ uvařený = PL ugotowany

## Adverbial Participles (Přechodníky / Imiesłowy przysłówkowe)

These exist in Slavic but NOT in German. They compress two actions into one sentence:

### Present adverbial participle (while doing X)
| Language | Formation | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| CZ | -e/-ě/-íc (archaic/literary) | Čta knihu, usnul. (While reading, he fell asleep.) |
| PL | -ąc | Czytając książkę, zasnął. | ✅ common! |
| RU | -я/-а | Читая книгу, он заснул. | ✅ common! |
| DE | ❌ (use "während" clause) | Während er las, schlief er ein. |

### Past adverbial participle (having done X)
| Language | Formation | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| CZ | archaic (nearly extinct) | Přečet knihu, šel spát. |
| PL | -wszy/-łszy | Przeczytawszy książkę, poszedł spać. | literary |
| RU | -в/-вши | Прочитав книгу, он пошёл спать. | ✅ common! |
| DE | ❌ | Nachdem er das Buch gelesen hatte, ging er schlafen. |

> **Key insight:** CZ has almost LOST adverbial participles — they sound archaic/literary.
> PL still uses present adverbial participles (-ąc) commonly.
> RU uses BOTH present and past adverbial participles frequently — they're essential in Russian.
> DE doesn't have them at all — uses subordinate clauses instead.

## Short-Form Adjectives (Russian only)

Russian has unique SHORT forms of adjectives used as predicates:

| Full form | Short form | Usage |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| красивый (beautiful) | красив/красива/красиво | Она **красива**. (She is beautiful.) |
| готовый (ready) | готов/готова/готово | Я **готов**. (I am ready.) |
| занятый (busy) | занят/занята/занято | Он **занят**. (He is busy.) |
| свободный (free) | свободен/свободна | Я **свободен**. (I am free.) |
| уверенный (sure) | уверен/уверена | Я **уверен**. (I am sure.) |

> Czech, Polish, and German don't have this short/long distinction. In Russian, short forms are ONLY used as predicates (after "to be"), never as attributive adjectives before nouns.

## German Partizip I and II Summary

| | Partizip I (present) | Partizip II (past) |
|--|---------------------|-------------------|
| Formation | stem + **-end** | **ge-** + stem + **-t/-en** |
| Example | les**end** | **ge**les**en** |
| As adjective | der les**ende** Mann | der **ge**les**ene** Brief |
| In compound tense | ❌ (not used) | ✅ Ich habe **gelesen**. |
| With werden (passive) | ❌ | ✅ Es wird **gelesen**. |

> German Partizip II is the workhorse — used for Perfekt tense AND passive voice. Partizip I is only used as an adjective.

# Research Background — Why This Approach Works

## The Science Behind Cross-Linguistic Transfer

### What is CLI?
Cross-Linguistic Influence (CLI) is the phenomenon where knowledge of one language affects learning of another. This can be positive (helpful transfer) or negative (interference). This vault is designed to maximize positive transfer and explicitly flag interference risks.

### Key Research Findings

#### 1. Multilingual Advantage
Research shows that people who already speak multiple languages learn additional languages faster than monolinguals. Multilinguals develop superior metalinguistic awareness — they're better at recognizing patterns, analogies, and structural similarities across languages.

#### 2. Cognate Awareness Matters
Studies on Polish multilingual learners found that those who know several languages are significantly more aware of cognate vocabulary and actively use this awareness when learning. Crucially, this awareness can be trained — even bilinguals who didn't initially notice cognates improved dramatically after explicit awareness-raising.

#### 3. Language Proximity Effect
Research consistently shows that typologically closer languages produce more transfer. For you: Czech→Polish transfer is massive (~80% vocabulary overlap). Czech→Russian is strong (~60%). English→German is strong within Germanic family. The further apart languages are, the more you rely on universal strategies rather than direct transfer.

#### 4. The EuroCom Method ("Seven Sieves")
The EuroCom project developed a systematic approach to leveraging cross-linguistic knowledge. Its "Seven Sieves" filter knowledge through: international vocabulary, pan-family vocabulary, sound correspondences, spelling/pronunciation, syntactic structures, morphosyntactic elements, and prefixes/suffixes. This vault's pattern notes are directly inspired by this approach.

#### 5. Associative Network Model
Vocabulary in the brain is not stored as isolated items but as interconnected networks. Words are linked by meaning (semantic associations), sound (phonological associations), and form (morphological associations). By explicitly creating these links across languages in Obsidian, you're mirroring how the brain naturally organizes multilingual knowledge.

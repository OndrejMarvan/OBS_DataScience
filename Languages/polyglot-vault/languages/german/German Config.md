# 🇩🇪 German — Language Configuration

## Language Info
- **Language:** German (Deutsch)
- **ISO Code:** de
- **Language Family:** Indo-European → Germanic → West Germanic
- **Script:** Latin (with ä, ö, ü, ß)
- **Current Level:** A2 (rebuilding)
- **Target Level:** B2+

## Relationship to Your Languages

| Compared to | Family Distance | Helpful For | Watch Out For |
|-------------|----------------|-------------|---------------|
| 🇨🇿 Czech | Different branch, but centuries of contact | Many German loanwords in Czech (švagr, knedlík, šunka, plech, flaška), case system concept | V2 word order, articles, very different verb system |
| 🇬🇧 English | Same branch (Germanic) | Massive cognate base (Wasser/water, Haus/house, Buch/book), similar basic vocabulary | German keeps more complexity (cases, gender, V2 order) |
| 🇵🇱 Polish | Different branch, historical contact | German loanwords in Polish (dach, ratusz, rynek, handel, cegła, szynka) | Grammar works very differently despite shared loanwords |
| 🇷🇺 Russian | Distant | Some international vocabulary | Very different in almost everything |

## Key Features
- **Cases:** 4 (Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ, Genitiv) — think of it as a simplified version of Czech/Polish!
- **Genders:** 3 (der/die/das = maskulin/feminin/neutral) — MUST learn article with each noun
- **Verb aspects:** No! (Unlike Slavic — this is actually simpler)
- **Word order:** V2 in main clauses, verb-final in subordinate clauses — this is the hardest part
- **Articles:** Definite (der/die/das) and indefinite (ein/eine) — doesn't exist in Czech/Polish!

## Pronunciation Key Points
- **ch** = [x] after a/o/u (like CZ ch), [ç] after e/i/consonants (new sound!)
- **ü** = [y] — doesn't exist in Czech/Polish (round your lips for 'i')
- **ö** = [ø] — doesn't exist in Czech/Polish (round your lips for 'e')
- **r** = varies by region, often uvular [ʁ] — very different from CZ/PL trilled r
- **Stress:** Usually on first syllable (like Czech!), except prefixes be-, ge-, er-, ver-, zer-, ent-, emp-
- **Long vowels:** Marked by doubling (Haar), silent h (Bahn), or ie (Bier)

## Bridge Patterns from English
1. EN **water** → DE **Wasser** (t→ss)
2. EN **book** → DE **Buch** (k→ch)
3. EN **make** → DE **machen** (k→ch)
4. EN **day** → DE **Tag** (d→t at start)
5. EN **apple** → DE **Apfel** (pp→pf)
6. EN **ship** → DE **Schiff** (sh→sch)
7. EN **sleep** → DE **schlafen** (sl→schl)
8. EN **pound** → DE **Pfund** (p→pf)

## Learning Priority
1. First focus: Articles (der/die/das) — learn every noun with its article, no exceptions
2. Then: V2 word order and subordinate clause verb-final rule
3. Then: Separable/inseparable verbs (an·rufen, auf·stehen, etc.)

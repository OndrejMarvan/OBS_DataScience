# Polish-Specific Grammar for Czech Speakers

## 1. The Pan/Pani System — More Formal Than Czech Vy

Polish formality uses **3rd person** with Pan (Mr) / Pani (Mrs), not 2nd person plural like Czech Vy.

| Register | Czech | Polish | German |
|----------|-------|--------|--------|
| Informal | Jak se máš? (2sg) | Jak się masz? (2sg) | Wie geht's dir? (2sg) |
| Formal | Jak se máte? (2pl) | Jak się Pan/Pani ma? (3sg!) | Wie geht es Ihnen? (3pl) |
| Plural formal | Jak se máte? | Jak się Państwo mają? (3pl) | Wie geht es Ihnen? |

### Examples
| CZ (Vy = 2nd pl) | PL (Pan/Pani = 3rd sg) | Literal PL |
|-------------------|----------------------|------------|
| Odkud **jste**? | Skąd **Pan jest** / Skąd **Pani jest**? | Where is Sir/Madam from? |
| Můžete mi pomoci? | Czy **Pan może** / **Pani może** mi pomóc? | Can Sir/Madam help me? |
| Co **chcete**? | Co **Pan chce** / **Pani chce**? | What does Sir/Madam want? |
| Jak se **jmenujete**? | Jak **Pan/Pani** się nazywa? | How does Sir/Madam call himself? |

> ⚠️ **This is the BIGGEST grammar surprise for Czech speakers.** In CZ, you just switch ty→Vy. In PL, you use a completely different grammatical person AND insert Pan/Pani.
> Polish is considered the most formally elaborate of all Slavic languages.

## 2. Masculine Personal Plural — Polish Exclusive Feature

Polish distinguishes **masculine personal** (virile) vs **non-masculine personal** (non-virile) in plural. Czech doesn't have this split as rigidly.

| | Masculine personal (men/mixed) | Non-masculine personal (women, things, animals) |
|--|-------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Nominative | ci dobrzy studenci | te dobre studentki |
| Past tense | oni **robili** | one **robiły** |
| Demonstrative | **ci** | **te** |
| Adjective ending | -i/-y (soft) | -e |

### Verb past tense comparison
| Subject | Czech | Polish |
|---------|-------|--------|
| They (men) went | Oni **šli** | Oni **szli** / **poszli** |
| They (women) went | Ony **šly** | One **szły** / **poszły** |
| They (things) fell | — **spadly** | — **spadły** |

> Czech distinguishes oni/ony in past tense too, but Polish extends this to adjectives, demonstratives, and numeral agreement. It's more pervasive.

## 3. Different Conjugation Endings

Czech and Polish verbs follow the same pattern but with different endings:

### Present tense comparison
| Person | CZ -at class (dělat) | PL -ać class (robić) | CZ -it class (mluvit) | PL -ić class (mówić) |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| já/ja | děl**ám** | robi**ę** | mluv**ím** | mówi**ę** |
| ty | děl**áš** | robi**sz** | mluv**íš** | mówi**sz** |
| on/ona | děl**á** | robi (Ø!) | mluv**í** | mówi (Ø!) |
| my | děl**áme** | robi**my** | mluv**íme** | mówi**my** |
| vy/wy | děl**áte** | robi**cie** | mluv**íte** | mówi**cie** |
| oni | děl**ají** | robi**ą** | mluv**í** | mówi**ą** |

> Key differences: PL 1st sg ends in **-ę** (nasal!), CZ ends in **-ám/-ím**.
> PL 3rd sg has **zero ending** (robi, mówi), CZ has **-á/-í**.
> PL 3rd pl ends in **-ą** (nasal!), CZ in **-ají/-í**.

## 4. Spelling Differences That Follow Rules

| Sound | Czech | Polish | Examples |
|-------|-------|--------|----------|
| [ʃ] | š | sz | škola → szkoła |
| [ʒ] | ž | ż/rz | žena → żona, řeka → rzeka |
| [tʃ] | č | cz | čas → czas |
| [v] | v | w | voda → woda |
| [j] after vowel | j | j | dej → daj |
| hard [l] | l | ł | — → łódź |
| soft consonants | ť/ď/ň | ć/dź/ń (or ci/dzi/ni) | — |

### When PL uses rz vs ż
| PL spelling | When? | CZ equivalent | Example |
|-------------|-------|---------------|---------|
| **rz** | Where CZ has **ř** | ř | rzeka (řeka), trzeba (třeba) |
| **rz** | After certain consonants | — | krzak, grzyb, brzeg |
| **ż** | Where CZ has **ž** | ž | żona (žena), żaba (žába) |
| **ż** | Other positions | — | nóż, już |

> **Knowing Czech ř helps you spell Polish!** If the Czech cognate has ř, Polish will have rz. If Czech has ž, Polish will have ż. This eliminates one of the biggest spelling headaches in Polish.

## 5. Preposition Traps

| Meaning | Czech | Polish | ⚠️ Trap |
|---------|-------|--------|---------|
| with (instrumental) | **s/se** + ins | **z/ze** + ins | Different word! CZ s ≠ PL z |
| from | **z/ze** + gen | **z/ze** + gen | Same word but CZ also uses "s" for "with"! |
| in order to | **aby/žeby** | **żeby/aby** | Same but reversed frequency |
| because | **protože** | **ponieważ/bo** | Different words entirely |
| although | **ačkoli** | **chociaż** | Different words |
| however | **ale/však** | **ale/jednak** | CZ však ≈ PL jednak |

> **The biggest trap:** CZ "s" (with) = PL "z" (with). But CZ "z" (from) = PL "z" (from) too!
> So PL "z" means BOTH "with" and "from" — context and case tell you which.
> "z przyjacielem" (with a friend, instrumental) vs "z miasta" (from the city, genitive)

## 6. Word Order Nuances

While both CZ and PL have free word order, there are subtle differences:

| Feature | Czech | Polish |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Adjective + noun | dobrý student | dobry student (same ✅) |
| Numeral position | tři studenti | trzech studentów (same ✅) |
| Clitic position | Strict 2nd position | More flexible |
| Question particle | Optional (jestli) | Common (czy) |
| Negative pronoun | nikdo **ne**přišel | nikt **nie** przyszedł (separate!) |

> CZ fuses negation: **ne**přišel. PL keeps it separate: **nie** przyszedł. Minor but consistent difference.

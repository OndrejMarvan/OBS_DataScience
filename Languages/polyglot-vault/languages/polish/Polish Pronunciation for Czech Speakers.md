# Polish Pronunciation for Czech Speakers

## The Good News
Czech and Polish share most consonant sounds. The spelling is different but the sounds are mostly the same. Here's your complete mapping:

## Consonant Spelling Map: CZ → PL

| Sound | 🇨🇿 Czech spelling | 🇵🇱 Polish spelling | Example CZ → PL |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| [ʃ] (š) | **š** | **sz** | šest → sześć |
| [ʒ] (ž) | **ž** | **ż** or **rz** | žena → żona, řeka → rzeka |
| [tʃ] (č) | **č** | **cz** | čas → czas |
| [dʒ] (dž) | **dž** | **dż** or **drz** | džbán → dżban |
| [ɲ] (ň) | **ň** | **ń** or **ni** | koň → koń |
| [c] (ť) | **ť** | **ć** or **ci** | — | different distribution |
| [ɟ] (ď) | **ď** | **dź** or **dzi** | — | different distribution |
| [x] (ch) | **ch** | **ch** | chléb → chleb ✅ same! |
| [v] | **v** | **w** | voda → woda |
| [w] | — | **ł** | — → łódź | ⚠️ Polish ł = English w! |
| [j] | **j** | **j** | jak → jak ✅ same! |
| [r] | **r** | **r** | ryba → ryba ✅ same! |
| [r̝] | **ř** | — | ⚠️ Polish doesn't have ř! |

### Key takeaways:
1. **š→sz, č→cz, ž→ż** — same sounds, just different letters. This is the biggest spelling difference.
2. **v→w** — Czech v = Polish w. Simple swap.
3. **ř→rz** — Czech ř sound doesn't exist in Polish. Polish rz is pronounced as [ʒ] (= ž/ż).
4. **Polish ł = [w]** — This sound doesn't exist as a letter in Czech. It's like English "w".

## Vowels — What's New in Polish

### Nasal vowels ą and ę (DON'T exist in Czech!)
- **ą** = nasal o [ɔ̃] — like French "on". Before stops (t,d,k,g): sounds like [om/on/oŋ]
- **ę** = nasal e [ɛ̃] — like French "in". Before stops: sounds like [em/en/eŋ]
- **Word-final ę** is usually just pronounced [e] in casual speech!

| Polish | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| mąka | [moŋka] | flour |
| ząb | [zomp] | tooth |
| będę | [bende] | I will be |
| język | [jenzyk] | language |
| idę | [ide] (casual!) | I'm going |
| piękny | [pjenkny] | beautiful |

### Polish ó vs u — SAME SOUND [u]!
Both ó and u are pronounced identically as [u]. The difference is purely historical/spelling:
- **ó** = where Czech has ou/ů (góra = hora, mój = můj)
- **u** = where Czech has u (usta = ústa)

### Polish y vs i
- **y** = [ɨ] — harder than Czech y, tongue further back
- **i** = [i] — same as Czech i, softens preceding consonant

## Stress Rule
**Always on the penultimate (second to last) syllable!**
- CZ: **PRA**-ha, **vo**-da (first syllable)
- PL: War-**SZA**-wa, wo-**DA** (penultimate)

This is the BIGGEST pronunciation difference from Czech. You need to actively move the stress.

| Word | CZ stress | PL stress |
|------|----------|----------|
| university | u-ni-**VER**-zi-ta | u-ni-wer-**SY**-tet |
| beautiful | **KRÁS**-ný | pięk-**NY** (2 syl) |
| I understand | **RO**-zu-mím | ro-zu-**MIEM** |
| thank you | **DĚ**-ku-ji | dzię-**KU**-ję |

## Practice Sentences
1. **Dzień dobry, jak się masz?** [dʑeɲ dobry, jak ɕe maʃ]
2. **Dziękuję bardzo** [dʑeŋkuje bardzo]
3. **Przepraszam, nie rozumiem** [pʃepraʃam, ɲe rozumjem]
4. **Gdzie jest przystanek?** [gdʑe jest pʃystanek]
5. **Chciałbym kupić bilet** [xtɕawbym kupitɕ bilet]

# The Fluency Method — How to Use This Vault

## The honest truth first

**Reading vocabulary tables does not create fluency.** If it did, everyone with a dictionary would be a polyglot. This vault contains ~4,800 words in cross-linguistic tables — but they only become *yours* through a cycle of encounter → anchor → retrieve → use.

Fluency = **automatic retrieval under time pressure**. That's trained by:

1. **Input** (listening/reading real content) — 60% of your time
2. **Spaced retrieval** (SRS review) — 20% of your time
3. **Output** (speaking/writing) — 20% of your time

The vault's job is to make each of these dramatically more efficient for *you specifically* — because every entry maps new words onto Czech, which is how your brain already stores them.

## The Core Loop (this is the whole method)

```
1. CONSUME real content in target language (podcast, article, video, conversation)
        ↓
2. NOTICE 3-5 words/phrases you didn't know or couldn't produce
        ↓
3. LOOK THEM UP in this vault (Vocabulary Index by Theme, or Ctrl+Shift+F search)
   → Found? Read the cross-linguistic row. Note the CZ anchor and any pattern link.
   → Not found? Create a word note from the template. YOU just extended the vault.
        ↓
4. The word note feeds SPACED REPETITION automatically (#review tag)
        ↓
5. USE the word within 48 hours: write a sentence, say it aloud, use it in a conversation
```

That's it. The 110 reference files exist so that step 3 takes 30 seconds instead of 10 minutes, and so every lookup *strengthens* connections to languages you already know instead of creating an isolated fact.

## What the reference tables ARE for

- **Pre-loading a topic:** Going to a Polish doctor next week? Skim [[A2-B1 Body Extended and Medical]] the day before. You're not memorizing — you're *priming*, so real encounters stick.
- **Pattern recognition:** When a Polish word feels familiar, the pattern files ([[CZ h - PL g - RU г]], [[Slavic Vowel Shifts]]) tell you *why* — and that "aha" is what makes it permanent.
- **Danger checking:** Before trusting a familiar-looking word, check [[False Friends Master List]].
- **Grammar reference:** When you produce a sentence and aren't sure about a case or word order, the grammar comparisons give you the answer *anchored to Czech*.

## What the tables are NOT for

- ❌ Reading top to bottom like a textbook
- ❌ Trying to "finish" a file
- ❌ Making flashcards of all 4,800 entries at once (you'll drown; SRS works only for words you've *met* in context)

## Input sources per language (the missing 60%)

| Language | Beginner-friendly | Your level |
|----------|------------------|------------|
| 🇵🇱 Polish | — | Real content: podcasts (e.g. news podcasts), Polish YouTube, Netflix with PL audio + PL subtitles, books you've already read in Czech |
| 🇩🇪 German | Slow-German-style podcasts, Nicos Weg (free A1-B1 course), graded readers | Then: Tagesschau in einfacher Sprache, German YouTube |
| 🇷🇺 Russian | Cyrillic first ([[Russian Config]] map), then slow Russian podcasts, RussianPod-style audio, dual-subtitle video | Comprehensible-input channels for beginners |

**The single highest-leverage trick for you:** read/watch things in Polish or Russian that you already know in Czech (translated Czech books, dubbed films you've seen). Your brain fills gaps from memory, so you stay in the "understand 95%" zone where acquisition happens.

## Output — the part everyone skips

Twice a week minimum, per your weekly rotation:
- **Write:** 3-5 sentences about your day in the focus language. Use words from this week's SR reviews. Check yourself against the grammar files after, not before.
- **Speak:** shadow a podcast for 2 minutes (repeat aloud in real time), or record a 1-minute voice memo describing something.
- **Best of all:** conversation — a tandem partner, a tutor (even 30 min/week), or speaking with Claude in the target language.

## Measuring progress (so you never feel lost again)

Not "how many files have I read" but:
1. **Word notes created by me** (Dataview counter on [[🏠 HOME]]) — target: 5-10/week
2. **SR retention rate** — target: >85%
3. **Monthly self-test:** speak 2 minutes into a recorder on a random topic. Save it. Compare with last month's.

If those three move, you are moving — regardless of how much of the vault you've "covered."

# B1 — Reflexive Verbs Across Languages

Reflexive verbs (with se/się/sich/ся) are MUCH more common in Slavic and German than in English. Many verbs that are reflexive in one Slavic language are also reflexive in the others — but not always!

## Tricky Cases: Reflexive in One Language, Not in Others
| Meaning | CZ | PL | DE | RU | Surprise? |
|---------|----|----|----|----|-----------|
| to sit down | sednout **si** | usiąść | **sich** setzen | сесть | CZ uses si (dative refl!) |
| to lie down | lehnout **si** | położyć **się** | **sich** hinlegen | лечь | CZ si, PL się, DE sich |
| to stand up | vstát | wstać | aufstehen | встать | NOT reflexive in any! |
| to hurry | spěchat | śpieszyć **się** | **sich** beeilen | торопиться/**ся** | CZ NOT reflexive, others ARE |
| to remember | pamatovat **si** | pamiętać | **sich** erinnern | помнить | CZ si (dative), DE sich, PL+RU not refl |
| to dream | zdát **se** | śnić (**się**) | träumen | сниться | CZ+PL reflexive, DE+RU not |
| to meet | setkat **se** | spotkać **się** | **sich** treffen | встретить**ся** | ALL reflexive! |
| to rest | odpočívat/**si** | odpoczywać | **sich** ausruhen | отдыхать | varies |
| to complain | stěžovat **si** | skarżyć **się** | **sich** beschweren | жаловаться | ALL reflexive! |
| to wonder | divit **se** | dziwić **się** | **sich** wundern | удивляться | ALL reflexive! |

> **Key insight:** ~70% of CZ reflexive verbs are also reflexive in PL. ~60% match German. ~50% match Russian. So CZ reflexivity is a decent predictor, but always verify — there are enough exceptions to trip you up.
> **CZ si vs se:** Czech distinguishes accusative reflexive "se" from dative reflexive "si" (pamatovat si, sednout si). Polish always uses "się". German always uses "sich". Russian uses "-ся/-сь" suffix.

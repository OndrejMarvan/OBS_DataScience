# B1 — Intermediate (Threshold)

## Goal
Understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters (work, school, leisure). Deal with most situations likely to arise while traveling. Produce simple connected text on familiar topics. Describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes, ambitions. Give reasons and explanations.

**Vocabulary target:** ~2500 words (cumulative)
**Duration:** ~4-6 months

## Grammar — B1

### Core Grammar Points
1. [[B1-G01 Conditional Mood]] — gdybym, gdybyś... + past tense
2. [[B1-G02 Imperative Mood]] — commands, requests, advice
3. [[B1-G03 Aspect in Depth]] — choosing perfective vs imperfective in all tenses
4. [[B1-G04 Verbs of Motion]] — iść/chodzić, jechać/jeździć, biec/biegać
5. [[B1-G05 Participles]] — present and past participles
6. [[B1-G06 Passive Voice]] — być + past participle, się constructions
7. [[B1-G07 Relative Clauses]] — który, która, które
8. [[B1-G08 Reported Speech]] — że + tense shifting
9. [[B1-G09 Complex Sentences]] — chociaż, mimo że, żeby, aby, zanim, odkąd
10. [[B1-G10 Numerals with Cases]] — complex numeral-noun agreement (2-4, 5+)
11. [[B1-G11 Diminutives and Augmentatives]] — -ek/-ka/-ko, -isko
12. [[B1-G12 Word Formation]] — prefixes and suffixes to build new words

### Cross-linguistic transfer notes for B1
> **Aspect mastery:** Czech and Polish aspect works almost identically — prefix patterns (na-, wy-, za-, do-, po-) often have the same meaning. This is your biggest advantage over non-Slavic learners.
> **Verbs of motion:** Czech jít/chodit, jet/jezdit → Polish iść/chodzić, jechać/jeździć — same system, slightly different forms.
> **For German:** Conditional uses würde + infinitive (simpler than PL). No aspect system — this is a relief but also means you need to express duration/completion differently.
> **For Russian:** Verbs of motion are more complex in Russian (3+ pairs), but the underlying logic is the same as Czech/Polish.
